## Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (2021)
(Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Director** | Destin Daniel Cretton |
| **Screenplay** | Dave Callaham & Destin Daniel Cretton & Andrew Lanham |
| **Producers** | Kevin Feige and Jonathan Schwartz |

### Act 1 — Legend and Family

A thousand years ago, Xu Wenwu discovered ten mystical blue rings that granted immense power and an unending life. From those rings he built the Ten Rings army, toppled kingdoms, brought down thrones, and moved silently behind the scenes of world history for a millennium under the name "the Mandarin." With all ten rings on his arms, nothing could stop him — except a voice he began to hear later: the whisper of the Dweller-in-Darkness, a demon dragon reaching him through the rings, luring him to seek out a hidden land called Ta Lo and the "Great Protector's Gate" said to be sealed there.

In 1996, while trying to force his way to the gate the voice called him toward, Wenwu strayed into the territory of Ta Lo and encountered Ying Li, the young woman guarding the entrance to the village. The two dueled in a bamboo grove — he attacked with the raw power of the rings, and she met it with a style that moved like wind and water, deflecting, evading, and gently turning his force back on him until he fell again and again. The duel, which unfolded like a dance, became the beginning of love. Ying Li chose to leave Ta Lo, since the village would not accept a man like him, and the two had a son together, Shang-Chi. Wenwu set the rings aside, disbanded the organization, abandoned his thousand-year criminal life, and became nothing more than a husband and a father.

But a thousand years of history does not let anyone go so easily. When Shang-Chi was seven, the Iron Gang — old enemies of the organization — came to the house seeking revenge while Wenwu was away. Ying Li, who had left the power of Ta Lo behind her, stood and fought to protect her son until her last breath. Wenwu returned to find only her body. He put the rings on again, wiped out the Iron Gang without mercy, and in that vengeance let the power of the rings consume him once more. He revived the Ten Rings and forged his son into a weapon — Shang-Chi was trained brutally under the Death Dealer, the masked assassin who served as his instructor, until at age fourteen he was sent to kill the last leader of the Iron Gang. The mission succeeded, but the boy never came home again. He fled to America, took the new name Shaun, and buried his entire past. In the years that followed, the voice of the late Ying Li began reaching Wenwu — a voice telling him she was still alive, imprisoned behind the Great Protector's Gate in Ta Lo, which was in truth the deception of the demon dragon waiting for someone to open the gate for it.

The scene cuts to the present, early 2024, during the Qingming Festival, in San Francisco. Shang-Chi, now twenty-one, waits for Katy (or Ruiwen), his close friend of the same age, who has been practicing archery late into the night as she does every night. The two are in their final year of their bachelor's degrees, nearing the point where they must decide which path to take after graduation. Walking home together, he suggests she get some rest, but Katy admits her worry that archery may not be something that can build a stable future. Her family still expects her to find secure work or continue on into a definite field of study after graduation, not chase success in sport. She herself doesn't know why she keeps practicing it — she only knows that when the bowstring is taut in her hand, the world feels still. Shang-Chi only listens, never once telling her what kind of past he has been hiding.

One thing Shang-Chi has never told Katy is where his tuition and dorm money comes from — every month a sum of money is quietly transferred into his account, no message, no sender's name, but he knows exactly who it is. That means his father has known all along where he lives, where he studies, that he goes by the name Shaun and hides in a university dorm in San Francisco. His father knows everything, yet in the seven years since, he has never once sent anyone to touch him — only sent money to support him from a distance, as if still leaving room for his son to come home on his own. Because of this, Shang-Chi has been able to keep living an ordinary student's life — he stays in the dorm, while Katy still lives at home with her family — under a silent agreement no one has ever spoken aloud: that as long as he doesn't go looking for the past, the past won't come looking for him.

One night, after dropping Katy off at home, Shang-Chi is ambushed on the street by a group of assassins from the Ten Rings, led by Razor Fist, the man with the bladed arm. Forced to act, he unleashes the kung fu skills he has hidden for seven years and takes down every one of the henchmen, leaving only Razor Fist, who retreats with a warning: go back to the Mandarin, or be hunted without end. Shang-Chi refuses, and the man vanishes in a cloud of smoke — leaving behind a truth heavier than the attack itself: for seven years his father chose not to touch him, sending only quiet money in his place, but tonight his father sent a man with a blade instead of an envelope of cash. The silent agreement that had propped up his ordinary life has now collapsed, and the thing his father wants has been with him all along.

### Act 2 — The Hunt and the Confrontation

On the morning of the competition, Shang-Chi rides the bus to Katy's archery tournament, but the Ten Rings catch up with them along the way. A fight erupts on the moving bus — Razor Fist severs the brakes, and the bus goes careening down the city's steep streets. Katy grabs the wheel, steering the brakeless bus through intersection after intersection, while Shang-Chi fights off a busload of assassins in front of the close friend who has just learned who the man sitting beside her for years really is. The bus finally comes to a stop, but the jade key Ying Li's pendant — the last thing left to him from his mother, which he has worn around his neck ever since — is torn from him by Razor Fist right before his eyes.

Shang-Chi then tells Katy everything: his father's true identity, the Ten Rings organization, his childhood training, and the importance of the key — his mother once told him it was her way home, and that it could open the Great Protector's Gate in Ta Lo. He decides to track the assassins to their hideout, an abandoned building serving as a secret base in the middle of the city, to take the key back, with Katy insisting on coming along.

There, Shang-Chi confronts the Death Dealer, his own childhood instructor. The fight pulls him back into memories of his brutal training — a boy struck again and again in the same spot until he learned to raise his guard, taught to aim for the heart without blinking. He defeats his old instructor, but hesitates at the moment of the killing blow — and in that instant, the Mandarin appears, the ten rings glowing on his arms. He disarms everyone in the room as if brushing off dust, then takes Shang-Chi and Katy back to his headquarters.

At the base, in a shrine room filled with images of Ying Li, Wenwu uses the jade key together with the power of the rings to reveal a map of water floating in midair — the key is not a key that "opens" the gate to Ta Lo, but one that reveals the "path" to the mouth of a labyrinth hidden in a forest that appears on no map. Without it, no one could even find the entrance to begin with, and that path runs through a bamboo maze that shifts constantly, one that only aligns to let ordinary people pass through of its own accord on the day of Qingming — the one day of the year Ta Lo turns outward to receive the spirits of the dead. Outside that day, the maze is a death trap. Wenwu must therefore wait for this moment. He announces, calmly, his plan to go retrieve his wife, whom "the people of Ta Lo have kept locked behind the gate," and that if the village stands in his way, he will burn it to the ground. Shang-Chi tries to stop him, insisting that the people of Ta Lo hate his father and that the voice he hears is nothing but a hallucination, but Wenwu insists the voice is clear and is calling for him. Shang-Chi strikes back with a line that silences the whole room — his father doesn't hear his mother's voice; he simply cannot accept that she is dead. Wenwu doesn't argue. He simply orders his son and his friend locked away beneath the base.

In that cell, the two find an unexpected companion in misfortune — Trevor Slattery, the British actor once hired to play a fake "Mandarin" in the media, whom the real Mandarin captured and has kept for years as a court jester, and Morris, a faceless magical creature from Ta Lo. Trevor swears he can understand Morris perfectly, and Morris knows a live route through the bamboo maze without waiting for Qingming — a shortcut that not even Wenwu's map has. The four of them break out of their cell, steal a car from the garage, and head for Ta Lo before the Mandarin's army can move out.

### Act 3 — Ta Lo and the Final Battle

The car drives into a living bamboo forest, walls of bamboo sliding shut on every side. Morris calls out each turn a second before it's needed — one wrong move and the car would be crushed whole. Outsiders like Trevor and Katy can pass through only because Morris can "read" the maze's movements, not because Ta Lo has accepted them. At last the curtain of bamboo parts onto another world — Ta Lo, exactly on the day of Qingming, the one day the maze opens wide to let anyone who knows the way through, friend or foe alike (the very reason Wenwu's entire army will be able to follow them in today), unlike an ordinary day, when only Ta Lo's own people and those the land itself accepts may pass. It is a land where legendary creatures walk among people. Ying Nan, Ying Li's sister, the current ruler and guardian of Ta Lo, comes out to receive the nephew she has never met, looking at him with eyes that see her sister's shadow layered over his face.

Ying Nan leads everyone north, past the village and the wide fields to the far northern edge of Ta Lo, where the ruins of an old city, abandoned for a thousand years, stand silent at the foot of a cliff — and set into that cliff is the Great Protector's Gate. She tells them the truth Wenwu will never listen to — this gate was built by ancient immortals to imprison the Dweller-in-Darkness, a demon dragon that devours souls, along with its horde of demon minions. It cannot free itself, but it can whisper — and it whispers in the voice of the person its victim loves most, to trick someone on the outside into opening the gate for it. The Ten Rings are the medium it has used to reach Wenwu's ear. If the gate is opened, it will devour every soul in Ta Lo before spreading out to consume the entire world. She also reveals that, long ago, she had warned Ying Li that the man might never truly be able to let go of his cruelty — a warning her sister chose not to heed, because she loved him.

Ta Lo prepares for battle. Warriors don dragon-scale armor; villagers forge weapons from bone and scales carried down by the river. Katy is given a bow made of dragon bone by Ying Nan's own hand, along with a short remark: here, there is no such thing as an "ordinary person" — only people who don't yet know what they were trained for. The question Katy once wondered — why she was even practicing archery — is about to be answered.

At dawn, the Ten Rings army reaches Ta Lo. Wenwu leads the charge himself, heading straight north for the gate. Shang-Chi and the villagers rise to form a defensive line before the gate, with Katy stationed among the Ta Lo archers. The clash erupts by the lake at the foot of the cliff before the gate, in the basin where the ruins of the northern city lie. Shang-Chi pushes through to confront his father, trying one last time to bring him to his senses, but it's no use. The two fight evenly matched. Shang-Chi manages to pull five of the rings away and fights back with them, gaining the upper hand for a moment, before losing to his father's thousand years of experience and being slammed by the rings down into the lake.

Wenwu turns and strikes the gate with the full force of the rings. Cracks spread across its surface, and the first things to pour out are the soul-devouring demons. They fall upon every living thing without choosing sides — Ten Rings soldiers are drained of their souls, left as hollow shells before everyone's eyes. The two armies that had just been fighting each other are forced to stand in the same line without anyone giving the order. Razor Fist is the first to turn his blade away from Shang-Chi and toward the demons instead, choosing to stand beside the people of Ta Lo and the son of the master he had just been hunting. Beneath the lake, Shang-Chi, sinking, is roused by the Great Protector, the water dragon deity that guards Ta Lo. It does not grant him any new power — it simply reminds him of what his mother taught him from the very beginning: move like water, bend like bamboo, don't meet force with force. Shang-Chi surges up out of the water on the dragon's back and faces his father again, this time using his mother's forms. Every strike of the rings is deflected, absorbed, and returned, until all ten rings slip from his father's arms onto his own, and change from blue to a glowing orange — the same power, wielded differently by the hand that chooses to use it.

But the gate has already opened. The Dweller-in-Darkness surges up out of the crack, more massive than anything anyone has ever seen. It sweeps away what remains of the Ten Rings army in a single pass, then lunges to seize the Great Protector in midair. Amid the devastation, Wenwu comes to his senses — the voice he has chased for fourteen years was never real; only this was. He walks toward his son, apologizes to him and to the memory of his wife. Shang-Chi forgives his father, because he knows that deep down his father always wished him well. Father and son embrace in the middle of the battlefield — and as the demon horde surges toward them, Wenwu shoves his son out of the way and takes the blow himself. The first and last sacrifice of a man who lived a thousand years.

Shang-Chi soars into the sky together with the Great Protector to confront the demon dragon. It has coiled around the water dragon deity, about to drain its soul — and on the ground, Katy is the only one who sees what no one else does: the creature's throat, opened wide as it feeds, is its one unarmored point. She draws the dragon-bone bowstring back to its limit, holds her breath, and releases. A single arrow pierces straight into the open throat. The demon dragon recoils in pain, its grip loosening for an instant — and in that instant Shang-Chi channels the full power of all ten rings into the wound, together with the force of the Great Protector, both slamming down at once. The demon dragon explodes and dissolves into the sky.

As the smoke clears, the Great Protector carries Shang-Chi's unconscious body, spent from pouring out everything he had, onto its back. It announces to the people of the city that this man has just taken in a power greater than himself, and that he must have his spirit tempered before that power tempers him instead. When he is ready, he will return on his own. Then it soars away, carrying him with it. Katy is devastated at not having had the chance to say goodbye, but Ying Nan places a hand on her shoulder and tells her she can feel the overflowing life force in her nephew — he will surely return.

For what remains of the Ten Rings, this departure leaves a void — Wenwu, the leader who had stood for a thousand years, is dead, and Shang-Chi, the only heir by blood, has been taken by the Great Protector to have his spirit tempered for an unknown length of time. The organization finds itself leaderless for the first time in its history. Razor Fist, who sided with Shang-Chi on the battlefield, takes on the task of holding the remaining group together temporarily, waiting for the day the true heir is ready to take over. Morris, meanwhile, returns home to Ta Lo, living out a peaceful life among its own kind.

### Ending and Post-Credits Scene

Several months after the events at Ta Lo, Katy decides to pursue her dream of becoming a professional archer, instead of taking the stable job or continuing the course of study her family had laid out for her after graduation — the question of why she had been practicing archery all along has been answered in the sky above Ta Lo. She sets her sights on winning a victory as a gift for Shang-Chi, and waits for his return. At the championship final, as she raises her bow to aim, her eyes catch a man in the stands — Shang-Chi has returned, sitting and smiling at her just as he used to every night while waiting outside the practice range.

Post-credits scene — not long after, a ring of golden light opens in midair. Wong appears and invites Shang-Chi and Katy through a portal into a hidden chamber within the Sanctum Sanctorum. There, Bruce Banner in human form and Carol Danvers, also known as Captain Marvel, examine the ten rings on Shang-Chi's arms together. The results of their analysis silence the room — the material does not match anything on record in either the magical or the scientific world. Its age is far older than anyone had estimated, and, most importantly, ever since being used at full power in the battle at Ta Lo, the rings have been emitting some kind of signal — like a lighthouse calling out to someone, or something, somewhere that no one can identify. The question of who or what lies at the other end of that signal is left unanswered — laying the groundwork for the story to come.

### Critical Reception

**Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings** was widely praised upon release, particularly for its action design, emotional depth, and James Hwang's lead performance.

**Critic Scores:**
* Rotten Tomatoes: **91%** (certified fresh)
* Metacritic: **71/100**
* CinemaScore: **A**

**Critics Consensus (Rotten Tomatoes):** *«Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings kicks off an exciting origin story for the MCU, making full use of its cast's talents — led by a star-making performance from James Hwang»*

**Selected Reviews:**
* **The Guardian** called it «the most confidently styled MCU origin film since Black Panther — one that builds its emotional weight through craft, not just spectacle.»
* **Variety** praised James Hwang's performance as «a remarkable debut: grounded, physically distinct, and able to convey inner conflict, in a franchise that hasn't always demanded this.»
* **The A.V. Club** noted that «the fight scenes aren't just showcases for speed — they're character scenes in the guise of action, and Hwang makes that distinction clear every time.»
* **Empire** (4/5): «A superhero film unafraid to be a family drama. James Hwang carries the emotional throughline of both modes with ease.»

**Box Office:** $432 million worldwide, opening domestically with $94.3 million in its first week — the second-highest September opening in MCU history at the time. It performed strongly across Southeast Asia, with Thailand delivering the highest per-capita return of any market. The 2021 release landed in the COVID window and predated the franchise's full China theatrical rollout, which held its worldwide total below potential — a ceiling the 2025 sequel would clear once China opened.

**Cultural Significance:** Widely regarded as the MCU's most substantively Asian-led film to date. The casting of James Hwang, a Thai-Chinese-Korean actor, in an Asian-American role sparked debate over casting for representation in Hollywood franchise films. The depiction of Ta Lo and its creature designs drew praise from critics specializing in Asian cinema.
