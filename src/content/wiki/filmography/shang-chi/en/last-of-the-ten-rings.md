### Act 1 — The First Month

Ying Nan's forty-nine-day rites end in the afternoon. The whole village stands on the training ground, burning joss paper in one shared brazier until the smoke rises in a column. Shang-Chi stands at the front as her only remaining kin, and he is the one holding the water bowl. Auntie Xiu is the one who hands it to him. Auntie Xiu is sixty-eight. She has cooked his breakfast every day since he came back to live in Ta Lo three years ago — that first morning she pushed two steamed buns on him even though he said he couldn't eat.

Afterwards the villagers haul the long table out to the middle of the courtyard and eat together until dark. Shang-Chi sits at the center of the table, not at the end. People refill his bowl. Someone teases him about patching a roof from the wrong side. A child snatches a chicken leg off his plate and runs. Three years ago he walked out of the Avengers base because he never felt like part of the place. That night at the long table, he thought he'd found where he belonged.

Ying Nan's things are all exactly where she left them. He goes into her house every morning, wipes the dust, changes the water in the vase, and comes out empty-handed. For the first three days he set a bowl at her place. On the fourth day the bowl was gone.

Katy has healed. Lately she spends nearly every day with a fifteen-year-old girl who snaps her fingers and makes small colored sparks bloom in the air. After dark she uses them to entertain the little kids. They still won't light a lamp. Nobody in Ta Lo thinks anything of it, because in a valley that used to have a dragon overhead and a man who wore ten rings walking its courtyards, a girl who makes fireworks is a party trick. Her real name is 李琚璧 — Lǐ Jūbì, two different kinds of ceremonial jade under the surname Lee. On their first day Katy asks for it, and the girl answers in the Ta Lo accent, which carries a sound somewhere in the middle that the outside world stopped using a long time ago. Katy tries three times and fails all three; on the third the girl laughs hard enough to set off two sparks by accident, so she writes it in the dirt with her finger instead. Katy can read exactly one character, 李, the other two being jades she has never seen before, so she guesses at the sounds and orders them the way a kid from San Francisco orders a name — surname last. It comes out *Jubi Lee*, and her mouth turns that into **Jubilee** on its own. The girl accepts it immediately and without negotiating, because in fifteen years everything she has ever had was something Ta Lo assigned to her and nobody had ever handed her anything to choose. Within a week the whole village is calling her Jubilee.

Katy teaches her archery every morning. Jubilee misses her first thirty shots. The thirty-first hits the edge of the target and she jumps so hard the sparks come out on their own. That day Katy teaches her something nobody in Ta Lo has: people without power don't lose because they have no power, they lose because they stop watching. That evening Jubilee asks what the outside world is like. Katy tells her about subways, karaoke rooms, snow that isn't mountain snow. Jubilee says she'd like to see it once. Katy says she'll take her.

Then the village's rhythm starts to slip. Doors close as Shang-Chi passes. The warriors move their training hours and nobody announces it. Two people stand at the northern path at all times, rotating every six hours; in one week he counts fourteen different faces. Nobody ever stops him from walking up there. There is simply always someone standing there, which means nobody has to. And Auntie Xiu's breakfast starts arriving later — seven o'clock becomes eight, eight becomes a bowl left at his door while she walks away. One morning he opens the door in time to see her back. He calls out. She turns, smiles at him, and keeps going. Meanwhile Guang Bo disappears north for two or three days at a stretch and comes back without saying what for. Shang-Chi asks once. Guang Bo says he went to look at the northern ridge, which answers nothing.

One evening Shang-Chi takes the satellite unit down from the shelf in his room. It is the only line that reaches through Ta Lo's seal to the outside. He tells Razor Fist to bring a force and camp at the bamboo forest — don't try to come in, don't send anyone in, just be there. Katy is in the room while he says it. When he puts the handset down she asks what he thinks is going to happen. He says he doesn't know, and then in the same breath tells her that if she ever can't reach him for more than a day, she is to walk out through the labyrinth and let the Ten Rings take her. Don't wait for him. Don't come looking. Katy asks what if he's wrong — what if the people here aren't thinking anything at all. He says if he's wrong, good. He'd like to be wrong.

That night he climbs to the lake. The Great Protector lowers its head close enough that he can see his own reflection in its eye. And then it says nothing. That silence is stranger than any answer, because every word it speaks to a human costs it — it has to lower the shield sealing Ta Lo for a moment. So for three years it has spoken briefly, one word at a time, in riddles. But it has never *not* spoken. Tonight it refuses to pay the price for even one word. It stares at him longer than it ever has, then turns, moves down into the lake, and sinks. The water is still within ten seconds. No waves. No bubbles.

The next night Jubilee knocks on Katy's window at two in the morning, hands shaking badly enough that the sparks at her fingertips gutter. She delivered laundry to Guang Bo's house at dusk and heard the people inside talking. They are all going to **seal** Shang-Chi. She tells Katy to leave tonight — tomorrow is too late. Katy asks who was in that house. Jubilee lists six names. The fourth is Auntie Xiu.

Katy doesn't pack. She walks straight to Shang-Chi and tells him everything that same night, including the fourth name. He listens all the way through and says nothing for a very long time, then tells her that tomorrow he's going to eat breakfast at the kitchen house the way he always does.

### Act 2 — The Setup

The next morning Shang-Chi gets to the kitchen house before anyone, and sits at his usual table. Auntie Xiu walks in and sees him. She stops in the doorway long enough for both of them to know the other knows. Then she goes to the stove, makes him rice porridge the way she does every day, sets it down in front of him, and stands beside the table. Shang-Chi asks if there's anything she wants to tell him. Auntie Xiu says Ta Lo has no water of its own, no rain of its own, no soil of its own — everything here belongs to the god. And she has lived sixty-eight years without ever having to think about that, until last month. Then she tells him to finish it all, and walks out. He finishes it all.

Later that morning Guang Bo invites him to the hall in the middle of the village. Two cups of hot tea are already set out. Three hundred villagers come and stand around the courtyard without anyone calling them. Guang Bo asks about Wenwu — how old his father was when he started to change, whether his father ever thought he was in the right. Shang-Chi answers each one politely, because he understands the questions aren't for him. They're for the three hundred people standing in the courtyard. Then he asks what Guang Bo is really asking. Guang Bo sets his cup down, stands, and says he'd like a match.

The first round is a straight test of internal force, palm against palm. Guang Bo pushes up level by level until the floorboards crack under both their knees. Shang-Chi holds it with nothing but his own strength and does not let out one flicker of dragon power — not because it's easy, but because he knows that the instant he does, three hundred people will see the thing they were brought here to see. Guang Bo pushes harder, then harder again, until blood seeps from the corner of his own mouth. That is the moment Shang-Chi is certain, because his uncle isn't testing him. His uncle is *trying*, and a man trying that hard is a man under orders. So he pulls his hand back, lets the force throw him flat, and stays down where everyone can see exactly who lost. There is no applause around the courtyard. Someone says quietly, you see, he wasn't going to do anything to anyone. Nobody else says anything.

On the other side of the village Katy tries to get into the courtyard and finds four warriors blocking the lane. She doesn't argue. She goes over the storehouse roof, crosses two buildings, comes down on the far side, and finds people waiting there too — which means it isn't crowd control, it's her. So she takes Jubilee north to the lake instead, because if this came down from above, the closest thing to *above* in Ta Lo is that dragon. The lake is empty, the water so still she can see the stones on the bottom. Jubilee wades in knee-deep and calls it in a kid's voice. Nothing answers. Katy stands on the bank and understands one thing: if the god who guards this place has run, then whatever's coming is bigger than the god who guards this place.

At the courtyard, Guang Bo stands over a man who won't get up, out of breath, his plan collapsed in front of the whole village. Then the curtain at the hall door opens and a man steps out with his right fist glowing gold to the wrist, and he drives it into Shang-Chi's ribs while Shang-Chi is still on the ground. The impact throws him through the hall wall. The man's name is Danny Rand. He tells Guang Bo, in the voice of someone who has waited too long, that this is enough — the Warden of the Land ordered a capture, not a piece of theater. Guang Bo shoots back that if the villagers don't see with their own eyes that this man is dangerous, they won't allow it, and he cannot command four hundred people who believe they're betraying a friend.

Shang-Chi climbs out of the splintered wood with three broken ribs and lets the dragon power out for the first time since the rings crumbled. The entire courtyard sees it at once. Guang Bo's plan succeeds in that second.

Danny comes first. The gold fist arrives in sequences — straight, fast, no tricks, the boxing of a man who has drilled one technique for ten years until there's nothing left to choose. Shang-Chi parries the first two with the outside of his arm; the third lands square on his chest and throws him into a stone wall, dust coming down the whole length of it. He gets up and changes approach: stop parrying, start steering. He pulls the gold fist into the wrong places — into posts, into rock, into the ground — because that punch is powerful but cannot be called back mid-flight. And because there are houses with people in them all around him, he walks Danny out toward open ground every chance he gets. They fight across three roofs, down onto the training ground, into an irrigation channel and back out of it, both of them soaked and bleeding.

Two minutes in, Shang-Chi catches something wrong: every time Danny's fist flares, he can feel a thread of power flowing into Danny from the north, from the direction of the lake. So he shouts at the sky, calling the Great Protector by the name Ying Nan taught him, and asks it straight out what it means to do. No answer comes — only Danny's fist burning brighter, which is the answer. But borrowed power doesn't lengthen a man's breath. Danny starts to labor, his timing drops half a step, Shang-Chi reads it, gets inside, locks the elbow, and is about to end it.

The building they're standing in explodes from the inside. A hundred warriors in ancient armor come down out of the sky and ring the courtyard on every side. The people of Ta Lo back against the walls without being told. And then **Erlang Shen** walks down out of the air with his celestial hound, third eye open.

Shang-Chi has fought a cosmic dragon and won. He has stood and taken Carol Danvers' whole assault and kept breathing. But both of those happened on ground where nobody was standing. Today he is in a village with four hundred people pressed against the walls around him, and the man in front of him isn't an angry dragon or a mistaken human. He is a god who has done this job for two thousand years and no longer has to think about it. Shang-Chi sets a feint — left shoulder, out right. The god's fist arrives before the left shoulder finishes moving. On the second beat he raises an arm to block and the arm breaks at the joint. On the third beat he's on the ground.

The overspill from that last beat carries past him into a hall pillar, and the pillar comes down. Jubilee is standing behind it — she came down from the lake because she wanted to know whether her warning had reached Katy in time. Guang Bo gets to the girl first, drops to his knees, shakes her, says her name over and over, then raises his head to look at Erlang Shen's weapon, still glowing. Auntie Xiu is second, but she doesn't run to the girl. She runs to Shang-Chi, kneels, puts her hands over his face, and shouts at the celestial soldiers not to take him. Danny Rand stands ten paces off, watching an old woman cradle the head of the man he was sent to collect.

They take Shang-Chi that afternoon. The satellite unit is still on the shelf in his room, because nobody carries a satellite unit to tea with their uncle. That night the villagers meet at the hall. Half the room says it's over, he's gone, there's nothing more to do. The other half says nothing at all. Auntie Xiu is the only one who stands up and asks: if he wasn't dangerous, then what exactly did we do this morning. Nobody answers her.

### Act 3 — Heaven's Cell

Shang-Chi wakes in a square stone room with no door and no window. There is light everywhere in it and no source anywhere for the light. There is a chain on his wrists, and the chain is not metal.

The Warden of the Land walks in with Erlang Shen standing guard behind him. This god wears the body of an old man in a worn official's robe and carries one register scroll. He opens by apologizing about the ribs, then tells him everything in the voice of a man who has told this story many times.

The story that Shang-Chi was a threat to Ta Lo is one he invented himself two months ago and sent down through Guang Bo. What he actually wants is the dragon power.

His reasoning is very short. Every god in the Court has exactly as much strength as the number of people still worshipping that god. Fewer worshippers, less strength; the day nobody remembers a god existed, that god is finished. Dragon power lives in the flesh and blood of whoever carries it. Nobody has to worship. Nobody has to remember. Nobody can cut off the supply. Ta Lo has four hundred people left, and those four hundred are all he has had for eight hundred years.

But he can't take it. Dragon power is bound to a body that still breathes; cut it out of someone who won't hand it over and the power dies with its owner. Ash either way. So he doesn't need to beat Shang-Chi. He needs Shang-Chi to give it to him.

Shang-Chi listens all the way through and asks one question, and it isn't about the power. He asks whether the woman who has cooked his breakfast for three years knew about this too.

The Warden says nobody in Ta Lo knows. They all simply do what the owner of the town tells them, because if they don't, next year there is no rain, no water in the streams, no soil that grows anything. And then he says one more sentence without realizing he's giving himself away: Ta Lo has belonged to him for eight hundred years, and the warmth Shang-Chi received for three of them belonged to him too. He simply hadn't charged for it yet.

Shang-Chi says nothing more until the god walks out of the room.

Katy waits the full day she promised, and then does not do what she promised. She starts at Jubilee's house. Nobody there, and the bed is folded too neatly for a house where an injured child slept last night. She goes to the apothecary; the old man there says someone came for the girl last night. She asks who. He doesn't answer. She asks again. He tells her to go home. She goes to the hall — locked. Guang Bo's house — empty. Finally she walks north along the path that has had watchers on it all month, and tonight there is nobody standing there at all, which is worse than someone being there.

At the end of the path is a wide stone platform she never knew existed in three years. A circle ten meters across is carved into the floor and the edge of it is still warm. There's a half-built wooden scaffold at one corner, and at the far end, a small body lying under a cloth.

The six celestial soldiers guarding it see her before she sees them. Six spears close in without anyone saying a word, because nobody thinks a woman with a bow needs to be talked to. Katy's first arrow doesn't go at a person. She shoots the rope holding the scaffold, and when the frame starts to lean she shoots the second rope. The whole thing comes down on the four standing nearest. The remaining two run at her along the only line available, because the platform has one way in and out. She backs onto a high rock and shoots down from an angle a four-cubit spear cannot reach. Nobody dies that night, and nobody gets up in time either.

She runs to the cloth at the far end and pulls it back. Jubilee, breathing, not conscious. Katy works it out in one pass: she can't carry her out alone. One way down, and ten more soldiers below.

She goes back to Shang-Chi's room, takes the satellite unit off the shelf, and calls Razor Fist to tell him the plan has changed. She isn't worried about Shang-Chi — a man with dragon power inside him doesn't die easily, and if they wanted him dead they wouldn't waste time carrying him somewhere else. The person nobody has gone looking for is a fifteen-year-old lying on a stone platform to the north. Razor Fist asks if he should send people in. She tells him they can't get in — not for lack of strength, but because that ground doesn't accept him.

That night she slings the unit over her shoulder and walks out through the labyrinth, passing through the way she has every time for three years. At the bamboo forest she spreads a map of Ta Lo she drew from memory across the hood of a truck, points at the stone platform, and says that if they want to get in fast enough, somebody has to open the seal from the inside. Razor Fist asks who's going to open it. She says she doesn't know.

In the cell there's a voice coming through the wall from the next room. Danny Rand has pried the air vent open, and he can do that because he isn't a prisoner. He's a contractor, free to come and go. He asks Shang-Chi straight out whether it's true he's going to destroy that valley. Shang-Chi says he's lived there three years, patched roofs for the people there, eaten at the same table as the people there — then asks what the man who hired Danny told him about it.

Danny is quiet for a long time, then tells his own story.

He was the only child of the Rand family, who ran a business in New York. When he was ten, the family plane came down in the mountains near Tibet. Both his parents died and he was the only survivor, and people from K'un-Lun took him in. K'un-Lun is one of the seven Capital Cities of Heaven, sitting in its own dimension and opening onto Earth once every fifteen years. He grew up there, trained there, won the trials to become its champion, killed a dragon by ritual, and earned this fist. After that he went back to New York to take his father's company back, and spent years there dealing with things that shouldn't have been in that city.

The reason he never went back to New York again is the day he returned to K'un-Lun and learned that the ruler who holds the title Yu-Ti — the man he had served since he was a boy — had been selling the way into the city to things that should never have been let in. Shang-Chi asks what he did. Danny says he walked out. Didn't bring anything down. Just walked out, and left everyone still inside with Yu-Ti for another ten years.

And the reason he's here is the same story. Yu-Ti owed the Warden of the Land a debt, and settled it by lending out the city's champion. He knows he was passed along like property. He took the job because what he was told when he took it was that a man was about to destroy a valley with four hundred people in it.

Then he asks Shang-Chi something: there are seven Cities, and he only knows six. Where is the last one. Shang-Chi says it's in the valley where yesterday he punched a man in the middle of a courtyard.

The next morning Danny brings water. Shang-Chi can't drink it with his hands chained, so Danny holds it for him, then asks how he's tried breaking this chain. Shang-Chi says three days, and the harder he calls the power the tighter it pulls. So Danny tries his own way — the gold fist at full force on the middle link. The chain doesn't break, but both of them see it shudder and hold for two seconds before going still. Shang-Chi says that while it shuddered, it felt thinner. Danny looks at the wall and says in that case the chain isn't made of metal. It's made out of its owner.

After that Danny is gone for two days. He walks out of heaven's precinct back to Ta Lo along the routes a contractor can use, stands and looks at the northern stone platform being prepared, listens while the villagers are called to the hall and told when the rite begins and where each of them is to lie down, and then comes back. He doesn't tell Shang-Chi what he saw. He only says that yesterday he asked the man who hired him what would happen to those four hundred people, and the man who hired him said he hadn't thought that far.

Above Ta Lo, the Great Protector is called up out of the lake to receive its last instruction. It was made to do two things at once: guard Ta Lo, and serve the god who owns Ta Lo. In a thousand years those two things had never once conflicted, so nobody ever had to ask which side it would pick if they did.

The instruction is to open the ground so the rite can draw up the souls of the whole town. Both sides are equally its duty, so it cannot choose, and it does the only thing still available to it — it stops being either one.

The dragon that lay above Ta Lo for a thousand years drives upward into the seal it has been holding in place and tears it open from the inside. The light through that tear is bright enough that the whole valley sees it at once, and its body dissolves along with the seal. No power passes to anyone. No last words. Only a hole the size of the sky, and the sound of four hundred people looking up to find that the thing above them their whole lives is gone. At the bamboo forest, Katy sees that light from the far side of the bamboo curtain and knows she has her answer about who was going to open the seal.

In the cell, the chain on Shang-Chi's wrists slackens. That chain was made from the god's power, and the god's power is bound to Ta Lo; the seal tears, the ground shakes, and the owner of the ground weakens with it. Danny Rand stands, walks to the wall, and punches it down. He could have walked out on his own since day one. What he does today is break a wall so somebody else can get out too.

### Act 4 — The Price of Four Hundred

Katy comes back into Ta Lo with a Ten Rings force that can follow her in for the first time — not because the ground accepts them, but because there is no seal left to refuse anyone.

The way into the village is blocked, and not by celestial soldiers. By the people of Ta Lo. Old men, young men, mothers holding children, standing in a line across the path and begging them to stop. Some are crying. Some can't look at her. They don't want to hurt anybody, but if they defy the owner of the town, next year there will be no water, no rain, nothing at all. Auntie Xiu is standing in the front row, and she is the only one not begging. She tells Katy shortly that the girl is on the northern stone platform, and steps back out of the line.

Razor Fist looks over the people blocking the path and tells his crew that last time they came here demons ran the whole unit down, and this time they're standing around waiting to get picked off by angels. He gives a hard order: nobody fires on civilians.

On the northern stone platform, the Warden of the Land has already begun, because Shang-Chi is out of the cell and he has no time left to wait. He raises the register and the souls of Ta Lo lift out of their bodies one at a time, hanging motionless in the air as four hundred small lights. The bodies below keep breathing. Their eyes stay open. They don't move.

But holding a soul outside its body is not free. Every minute it hangs there it burns itself down like a candle somebody lit and walked away from. The first to go are the old and the very young: the light dims, goes out, and the body underneath keeps breathing a while longer before it stops. The Warden hides none of this. He says it out loud, because it is part of the offer — the longer Shang-Chi takes, the smaller the pile waiting for him.

Guang Bo pushes through into the middle of the platform. For two months he has told himself one sentence — that he did this so nobody in Ta Lo would get hurt. Yesterday a child got hurt. Today the whole town is strung up in the air. The sentence he's been telling himself for two months doesn't work anymore. He shouts that this is not the arrangement — he agreed to help take one man, not to let four hundred people be used as collateral — and tears the guardian's mark off his arm and throws it on the ground in front of the god.

Erlang Shen takes one step forward and cuts him once. Guang Bo's body doesn't fall. It disperses. His soul scatters into fine light and goes out in mid-air. It doesn't rise to join the other four hundred, because those are being kept for the rite and his is no use to anyone now. Katy is eighty meters away. She sees all of it, and from that distance there is nothing she can do.

In heaven's precinct, Shang-Chi and Danny run along a stone corridor with no visible end. There is one route, and every stretch of it has someone waiting.

**Guan Yu** stands at the first junction, halberd planted in the floor. No threats, no sermon, only a question of whether they do this armed or bare-handed. Shang-Chi chooses bare-handed. The halberd is three times the length of a man's arm and its swing fills the corridor. For the first ten attempts he cannot get inside the arc — takes the shaft across the body twice and the edge across his shoulder once. Danny moves in to help and is thrown through a wall into the next room. So Shang-Chi stops trying to get inside and starts counting instead. He lets the halberd pass his face three times to see where the god's wrist turns on the fourth, then steps into exactly that gap, catches the shaft, and uses the god's own momentum to lift himself into a knee under the chin.

Guan Yu backs off two steps, blood in his mouth, and laughs. What follows is the most straightforward fight in the film — nobody deceives anybody, and both of them run out of strength. When Shang-Chi goes down with no chance of rising in time, Guan Yu lifts the halberd and sets it at his side. He says he came under orders, not conviction, and the man he was told to take didn't fight the way the orders said he would. Then he walks back the way he came. He doesn't take a side. He just doesn't finish the job.

**Nezha** is waiting in the next hall, standing on two wheels of fire with a fire-tipped spear, a full head shorter than Shang-Chi. For three minutes nobody can touch him — the wheels carry him around the ceiling and bring him down stabbing from angles nobody is covering. Shang-Chi bleeds from five puncture wounds. Danny gets his ankle hooked by the spear and is flung ten meters. Then Shang-Chi notices the wheels turn tighter to the left than to the right, so he stands with his right side open as bait, and when Nezha comes in along that line he kicks the spear out of his hands, locks his neck, and pins him to the floor.

Then he lets go and steps back two paces. Nezha gets up and looks at him for a long time before taking his wheels and leaving without reporting a defeat.

**Erlang Shen** is waiting at the last door. This time Shang-Chi doesn't bait, doesn't feint, doesn't deflect, because he knows the third eye reads all of it. His whole discipline is built on making an opponent believe something untrue, and when that's unavailable, all that's left is fighting straight.

For the first three minutes both of them lose. Shang-Chi is driven into the floor twice. Danny is caught by the throat and slammed into a pillar. The celestial hound has Shang-Chi's leg in its jaws and is dragging him backward. The turn comes when Danny shouts at him not to look at the eyes, because the third eye reads a man's gaze — so Shang-Chi fights the rest of it with his eyes shut. After that the two of them don't speak again for the whole exchange. Danny goes high, the god blocks. Shang-Chi goes to the body, the god blocks that too. But he cannot hold two lines forever. The hound jumps in to help and Danny punches it through three layers of stone wall. Shang-Chi is the one who takes the god's last punch full in the chest, and he takes it to open the range for Danny. The gold fist lands center-mass on the armor. The armor cracks. The god goes down on one knee for the first time in two thousand years.

The two of them push the door open, and that door opens onto the northern stone platform of Ta Lo. In front of them are four hundred bodies breathing and not moving, and the lights hanging motionless overhead no longer number four hundred.

Katy breaks through the villagers' line at that exact moment. She doesn't run to Shang-Chi. She runs to one small body at the edge of the platform, drops to her knees, takes the wrist — still warm, still beating. Shang-Chi scans for another body and finds Auntie Xiu in the third row, eyes open, looking at him, unable to move.

The Warden of the Land turns to him and makes an offer.

### Act 5 — The Owner

The offer is very short. Hand over the dragon power. Every light still burning goes back into its body. Ta Lo stays. Everyone not yet gone out stays.

Shang-Chi asks what happens if he refuses. The Warden does not threaten to kill anyone. He only says that he can wait — he has waited eight hundred years — and then points up at the field of lights and invites Shang-Chi to count them himself: how many went up, and how many are left. He is not the one who can't wait.

Someone said this same sentence to his father once, from behind a stone gate. His father complied, and his father lost everything.

Shang-Chi looks around. At the mountains he thought were home. At the house his mother grew up in. At the courtyard where he sat in the center of the long table a month ago. And he understands that not one thing in it belongs to him, not a single stone. All of it belongs to the old man standing in front of him, and has for eight hundred years.

What belongs to Shang-Chi is the people lying on the ground. And he already knows that if he chooses to fight, some of them will not be getting up again.

His first punch doesn't go at the god. It goes into the ground. The fracture runs from the stone platform to the foot of the northern ridge, and the god flinches for the first time since he appeared.

Danny says it out loud first: in K'un-Lun the ruler and the city are the same thing, inseparable. Put an arrow in the city wall and Yu-Ti bleeds. If this place was built from the same pattern, then the god isn't *in* Ta Lo. He *is* Ta Lo.

So Shang-Chi orders the Ten Rings to move everyone out to the bamboo forest. Every body. Nobody left behind. Razor Fist asks what to do about the ones whose souls are still in the air. He says carry them anyway.

The war opens on two fronts at once. At the mouth of the labyrinth Erlang Shen leads heaven's force against the evacuation, because if the people all get out the god has nothing left to trade. Against him: Katy shooting from high ground, aiming at the backs of knees where the armor doesn't cover; Danny Rand holding the entrance alone with the gold fist for half an hour; and Razor Fist and the Ten Rings carrying bodies out two at a time. Heaven's soldiers do not distinguish between who is armed and who isn't; the spears land on the carried the same as on the carriers. The longest line of that night is the line of people who never reached the mouth of the path.

When the fracture reaches under the stone platform, the carved circle cracks and the souls still burning begin dropping back into their bodies in waves — ten or twenty at a time, not all at once. The ones who get theirs back first are the ones lying closest to the crack. The ones that had already dimmed drop nowhere at all; they go out where they hang, and the people who run to shake the bodies below cannot wake them.

The first person to stand up is Jubilee, because her body has been unconscious since yesterday, so getting her soul back is the only thing that changes in her. She gets up into dust thick enough that people can't see each other at ten meters, with voices calling from every direction and nobody finding anybody. So she does the one thing she can do: climbs the highest pile of rubble and snaps her fingers. The sparks that won't light a lamp go up into the sky and are visible for a kilometer in dust that thick. She fires them in a pattern — three, pause, three, pause — telling people who can't see anything at all which way is out. The party trick nobody in Ta Lo ever cared about becomes the thing that gets them out.

Auntie Xiu gets her soul back in the third wave. She doesn't pick up a weapon and doesn't run to anyone. She walks to the mouth of the path and counts the heads going past, because somebody has to count. The villagers who can stand pick up weapons — the same people who an hour ago were blocking the way out, now blocking the way in against celestial soldiers.

At the center of the valley, Shang-Chi fights the Warden of the Land. This god has no martial training at all and never needed any, because for eight hundred years nobody in Ta Lo dared touch him. What he has is the whole of the ground. He raises the floor into a wall to block a punch, pulls rock off the ridge to use as a fist of his own, changes the direction of gravity in mid-air until Shang-Chi drops onto the ceiling of a cave. For the first ten minutes Shang-Chi cannot reach him even once.

So he stops chasing the god and starts breaking what the god picks up. The wall he just raised. The rock he pulled in. The floor he flipped. Every time it breaks, the god flinches, and every time the god flinches he loses a beat.

Then Ta Lo starts coming apart. The northern ridge folds in on itself. The sky splits into two layers moving in opposite directions. The field where he used to run with Katy lifts until it stands perpendicular to the ground. The courtyard where he sat at the center of the long table drops into a fissure. The house his mother grew up in tears loose, rises, and comes apart in the air. The god screams every time the ground splits, because he has no other body to hurt instead.

The splitting ground does not choose who is standing where. A fissure opens through the middle of the evacuation line twice, and the second one takes the whole line with it, Ten Rings men and the bodies on their backs together. Nobody stops walking to look, because stopping costs the line behind them.

The last person there was still anything left to carry is brought out through the labyrinth as the dimension begins collapsing inward from its edges. Shang-Chi shouts for everyone to wait at the bamboo forest, and not to worry about him. Katy looks back at him once from the mouth of the path, then walks out without saying anything.

With nobody left in Ta Lo, Shang-Chi stops protecting the ground. His last punch holds nothing back — not for the floor, not for the houses, not for himself. The dragon power leaves him all at once, in a single mass. The dimension collapses toward its center.

The Warden of the Land clings to his own ground until the final second, shouting the name of his office over and over because that office is all he ever had. Then a hand reaches down from above and plucks him out of the wreckage without interest, the way one collects evidence, not the way one saves a colleague.

He doesn't die. He is filed. And Ta Lo is gone.

### Conclusion and Credits Scenes

What Shang-Chi sees next isn't darkness. He is floating somewhere with no up, no down, and no time, and in front of him two universes are drifting slowly toward each other. Their edges have already touched, and at the point of contact everything is disappearing one layer at a time. Something speaks to him there — not a language, with no direction, not arriving in his ears but in his bones. It tells him that what he just tore down in a small valley was very small, and what is coming is not. Then several hands reach in and take him out of it. Not one pair of them is human.

He opens his eyes in a stone hall with no visible ceiling, still wet with his own blood. The Celestial Court is all around him, every one of them present. He hasn't been brought here to be punished. He has been brought here to be reviewed. **Nezha** speaks first, that he pinned a beaten opponent and let go. **Guan Yu** speaks next, that he fought like a man and not the way the orders said he would. Neither of them speaks because they like him. They speak because to stay silent would be to accept that the orders they took yesterday were correct.

They return his dragon power. Not out of mercy — the Makluan are extinct and he is the last vessel left in the universe, and what has just happened has taught the Court that if this power ended up with any one god, that god would stop needing worship, and the Court would no longer hold any authority over them. Safer in the hands of a mortal who can die. The judgment says more about the Court than about him.

Shang-Chi takes it back and tells them plainly that if they call him one day he may come or he may not, and nobody standing there has the right to know in advance.

Before they leave, he asks about the voice he heard between the dimensions. The Court is silent for an unusually long moment, and then one of them answers with a single name — **Wuji** — and explains only as much as it has to. That thing is not a god. It has no temple, nobody kneels to it, and it does not stand above the Court in any sense of rank, because it was never inside the same ranking to begin with. Gods are things that exist in the world; Wuji is the space the world sits in. The distance between one star and the next, the gap between him and the god speaking to him right now — all of that is it. And it has never existed alone. There is always a second one beside it, the one that is time instead of space, and both of them are older than every heaven men have ever named.

Shang-Chi asks why it spoke to him. The Court says it didn't. What he heard was the sound of something in pain, because the seam he saw is two spaces being pressed together, and when they press, the first thing crushed is that thing itself. That is why the voice arrived not in his ears but in his bones — he wasn't standing outside listening to it, he was standing inside it. What the Court does not answer, and visibly does not want to answer, is why something that large chose to be heard by one mortal instead of telling them. They only say: not yet, this is not his time. Gods will handle gods. What he saw at that seam is not a matter for the Chinese heavens, and it is not something any valley can hold back. They tell him to go deal with that instead.

**Erlang Shen** is the last one still standing. He hasn't changed his mind and doesn't apologize. He only says that if he is ever ordered back, he will come back. Shang-Chi nods, because he has heard this sentence before, from someone he respects.

After that there is no ceremony of any kind. The floor under his feet simply isn't there anymore, the way somebody leaves a drain cover off.

He lands on grass at the bamboo forest in the late morning, two hundred meters from the camp. In his own head, everything from his last punch in Ta Lo to being dropped out took less than one morning. What is in front of him is not something anyone finishes in one morning. There isn't a single tent left in the camp. There are eight timber houses with their roofs on, livestock pens, an irrigation channel dug deep enough that water is running in it, and two children he has never seen before running between the houses. At the back of the camp are four mounds of earth in a row, a wooden marker on each. Time up there and time down here have never run at the same speed, and it did not occur to any god that this was something he needed to be told.

Under those four markers are three elders and one infant who didn't survive the first season outside. The two children running are the ones born in the two months he was gone. Auntie Xiu is still here; she has cooked breakfast at the new kitchen house every day of it, and she is the only one who knows the exact figure, because she is the one who stood at the mouth of the path that night and counted. Eighty-seven, out of four hundred. She counted three times before she would believe her own count. Katy has cut her hair short. She is serving rice beside Auntie Xiu when someone starts shouting. Everyone had already accepted that he was dead.

The closing image is the camp the next morning. People queuing for rice. Children running on soil that belongs to no god. Jubilee sitting on a fence rail snapping sparks for the little ones — the same sparks, still not enough to light a lamp. Danny Rand comes over to tell Shang-Chi there are six more cities like that one, and that this time he isn't walking out alone. Auntie Xiu brings him two steamed buns and tells him to finish them. Razor Fist comes to stand beside him and asks what the orders are, because an entire organization is waiting on his word. Shang-Chi looks at eighty-seven people who have no home because of him and says the first thing is to find them somewhere to live.

That evening a group of elders comes to Katy's new house carrying a gift they bought at a market in town: a blank notebook. They tell her nobody in Ta Lo ever wrote anything down, because when the land is going to last forever it never occurs to anyone that they need to remember. The land is gone now, and the only person who wrote everything down for three years is the outsider they once told to go home. They ask her to write Ta Lo back — every legend, every rite, every name, including the name of the last guardian, of whom there was nothing left to bury. Katy looks down at the old notebook open on her lap and agrees. Then Jubilee comes and sits beside her and asks what the outside world is like. Katy says she'll take her.

**Mid-credits scene** — At Avengers headquarters, Shang-Chi sits across from Sam Wilson and asks for exactly one thing: help finding somewhere for his people to live. Sam asks how many. Eighty-seven, Shang-Chi says. Sam's shoulders drop an inch and he says that's a number he can work with. Two months ago it was four hundred, Shang-Chi says. No documents, no citizenship, no birthdates matching any calendar on Earth, from a dimension that no longer exists. Sam gets up, goes to the window, rubs his face, and looks down. Ten Rings armored vehicles are parked three blocks deep around the building. Armed personnel fill the plaza. Not one of them is doing anything. They are all just standing there, waiting, very politely. Sam closes his eyes for a moment. Then something occurs to him.

Cut to a fjord in Norway. New Asgard on the left of frame: Scandinavian timber houses, a fishing dock, Asgardian colors. And on the right of frame, two hundred meters along the shore, a new village with a Chinese gate, rows of red lanterns, and a carved wooden street sign at the head of the road that reads NEW TA LO. Valkyrie stands on the rise with a few other Asgardians, looking over at it for a long moment, before saying that those are the Ta Lo people, aren't they — and then asking whether their gods blew up their dimension too. Nobody answers her. Firecrackers go off from the direction of the new village — cut to black.

**Post-credits scene** — Los Angeles, daytime, but the sky is a color the sky should not be. Whole blocks of buildings are disappearing one floor at a time from the top down — not collapsing, not burning, simply gone, as though someone were erasing them. People fill the streets running. And above the western horizon an enormous curve is moving slowly closer. No caption tells us where this is.

Shang-Chi stands on the roof of an overturned car, hitting redial. No rings. No scales across his skin. Nothing glowing anywhere on him — only two bare hands, which handled four men two minutes ago. He calls the Avengers and nobody picks up. He calls again and nobody picks up.

A woman climbs onto the same car. Her face is Katy's in every detail, her voice is Katy's in every detail, but her hair is shorter, she's dressed nothing like her, and she has a gun in her hand. Shang-Chi calls her Leiko. She tells him she can't reach MI6 either, on the main line or the backup.

Sparks go off below them. Jubilee climbs up onto the car — a few years older than she was in Ta Lo, in a leather jacket, with far better control of the sparks in her hands. She says the X-Men just sent word. What's happening is called an incursion. Two universes are colliding, and nobody on this side has ever seen one before.

Then two men come through the dust. The first is in his early twenties, in an ordinary human body, carrying three tablets. His name is Amadeus Cho, and he says he's already calculated where the seam is thinnest. The second walks in ahead of everyone else — a middle-aged man in a suit that's already ruined. His name is Jimmy Woo, and he doesn't ask anyone what's happening, because he already knows. He tells them they need to get to the source, and starts walking without waiting for anyone to agree.

All four follow him into the dust. The camera holds on the emptying street. Then black, and one line of text.

**AGENTS OF ATLAS WILL RETURN**

### Production

| Phase | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Pre-production _(shared with Legacy)_ | Oct 2023 – Jun 2024 |
| Principal photography _(back-to-back block)_ | Dec 2024 – Mar 2025 |
| Additional photography | Jan – Feb 2026 |
| Post-production | Apr 2025 – Aug 2026 |
| Theatrical release | September 18, 2026 |

Legacy and Last of the Ten Rings were greenlit together in 2023 and shot back-to-back as a single continuous production — a bet Marvel had not placed since Infinity War and Endgame. Pre-production developed both scripts at once (Oct 2023 – Jun 2024), because a back-to-back block requires every page locked before day one: the schedule is built by set and location rather than by film. Principal photography ran unbroken from 11 July 2024 to March 2025. Legacy's material wrapped on 14 November 2024 and the camera rolled straight into the finale.

What made this block unusually economical is that the finale takes place almost entirely on the previous film's ground. The training ground, the village, the wooden houses, and the bamboo labyrinth built for Legacy were carried straight over — and then destroyed for real in the final act. Shooting Ta Lo's collapse meant striking both films' sets simultaneously, and it could only be done once. Fourteen cameras were placed for that day.

The economics are the point — one crew, one set build, one cast contract across two films — but so is the exposure: the finale was in the can before the middle film had earned a dollar. Post-production was staggered rather than shared. Legacy's edit and VFX pipeline opened in Dec 2024 while cameras were still rolling on the finale, and the finale then took a seventeen-month post tail (Apr 2025 – Aug 2026), which it genuinely needed: the god sequences and the collapse of an entire dimension made it the heaviest VFX load in the trilogy.

Cretton's schedule across those years was the most punishing of any MCU director's. He signed on to direct **Spider-Man: Brand New Day** in Oct 2024 while still shooting the Shang-Chi block in Sydney, because negotiating one studio picture while shooting another is ordinary in this job. When the block wrapped in March 2025 he had four months to prep Brand New Day before cameras rolled in Glasgow on 3 August 2025 and wrapped on 19 December 2025. The month after that he came back for the finale's additional photography in Jan–Feb 2026 — deliberately pushed to sit half a year after Legacy had opened, so the ending could be adjusted against real audience response. Then he carried Brand New Day's post through to June 2026, delivered it for a 31 July 2026 release, and had two months left to close out the final pass on his own trilogy's ending before 18 September. The price of doing all three was withdrawing from the Disney+ series **Wonder Man**, which had been part of his original overall deal with Marvel.

The block also compressed James Hwang's film work into one window. He shot the finale — the most fight-heavy film of the three — while flying between the Sydney stages and Asia for NCT 127's "Neo City — The Momentum" tour (Jan–May 2025), the most punishing stretch of scheduling in his career and one he has since described as the closest he came to breaking. It bought him the whole of late 2025 and 2026 for music.

### Creative Intent & the Reworked Ending

James Hwang had committed to Shang-Chi only through this film. The script he co-wrote was built to end it *Logan*-style — Shang-Chi burns off every trace of the dragon power to destroy Ta Lo and the god bound to it, then walks out of the trilogy as an ordinary man with nothing but his mother's kung fu: no power, no home, no way back.

Marvel refused, because the character was still needed for **Avengers: Doomsday**, which entered production more than a year before this film opened. The negotiation landed on the scene where the Celestial Court returns his power in its own hall before throwing him back down — with the writers holding two conditions. The restoration had to come from the mouths of the two gods Shang-Chi chose not to kill in Act 4, so it read as a consequence rather than a gift. And he had to come back late — late enough that the people waiting had stopped waiting, with four graves in the ground and two children born.

The seam of that compromise is still visible in the finished film, and there is a production reason underneath it: Cretton closed out Brand New Day's final in June 2026 and had two months left to finish the trilogy's ending before release. Several critics pointed straight at the last three minutes as the part most visibly made in the narrowest window. Critics split cleanly on it. One camp read the scene as the trilogy's most complete thematic capstone. The other read it as a studio-mandated reset button that undoes a sacrifice the film had spent two and a half hours building.

---

### The Doomsday Question

From 2025 onward Marvel publicly listed Shang-Chi among the returning heroes of **Avengers: Doomsday** — yet Legacy had just closed with him pushing his nameplate across the table, resigning from the team, and walking into Ta Lo alone. Because the finale had been shot back-to-back and was already locked in post before the question was ever asked, fans spent more than a year guessing how the bridge would be built — all the more so once **Spider-Man: Brand New Day** was positioned to take place *before* Doomsday, with Cretton confirming that its release-date move let it stand on its own without needing to connect to Doomsday at all — leaving the Shang-Chi finale as the only on-ramp anyone could see.

The answer the film gives isn't a bridge. It's an instruction from a god. The Celestial Court tells Shang-Chi outright that gods will handle gods, and that what he saw at the seam between two universes is not something any valley can hold back — and the only word they use for the thing that spoke to him there is a name, **Wuji** — a name that appears in no Chinese legend, and one that not a single god in that scene calls a god.

That scene became the talking point of the whole season, because the film says almost everything about the thing except the one name a Western audience would recognise. The most widely accepted theory points to Infinity. In Daoist cosmology, Wuji (無極) means the Boundless, the limitless state that precedes even Taiji — a word-for-word match for the comics' embodiment of space. And the second one the Court mentions, the one that is time and is always paired with it and is pointedly never named, matches the Eternity the MCU already put on screen in Thor: Love and Thunder. Read that way, the film isn't introducing one new entity; it is confirming in a single line that both time and space exist as beings in this universe. Comic readers push it further — Infinity has a history of choosing mortal champions as its instruments, which answers precisely the one question the Court refuses to answer, and its classic opposite is Oblivion, the embodiment of non-existence, which fuels its own round of speculation about what is coming. All the more so with Avengers: Secret Wars (2027) already slated after Doomsday: in the comics, Secret Wars is where every incursion leads, and the road there is littered with cosmic beings hunted down universe by universe. Which reframes the Court's line about the voice being something in pain — what came down to Shang-Chi may not be an observer at all, but something running for its life. Marvel has never confirmed or denied it.

### Critical Reception

**Production and creative context:**
Having been shot back-to-back with Legacy, the finale kept the trilogy's original creative team intact — Destin Daniel Cretton directing all three films, with James Hwang retaining a co-writing credit alongside Dave Callaham and Andrew Lanham. What changed was scale rather than personnel: Legacy is a film about a hero learning not to act, while the finale is one in which he takes on an entire pantheon and discovers the real enemy is the thing that makes other people bleed when you hit it. Cretton described it in interviews as "a war film where the hero has to burn down his own house to win." The credits scenes carry uncredited cameos from Anthony Mackie (Sam Wilson) and Tessa Thompson (Valkyrie).

Anthony Wong Chau-sang drew the strongest supporting notices as the Warden of the Land, for playing the god as an official past his expiry rather than a tyrant. Max Zhang's Erlang Shen was widely called the best opponent in the trilogy, while Vincent Zhao's single extended duel as Guan Yu was called the cleanest fight of the modern MCU — two fighters who respect the craft going until both are spent, with nobody deceiving anybody. Izaac Wang plays Nezha's insolence as equal parts irritating and endearing. The return of Yuen Wah as Guang Bo, from the first film, was read as the franchise's most rewarding long game. Deanie Ip's Auntie Xiu was singled out as the film's quiet heart, with several critics reaching back to the caretaker role in A Simple Life that won her Best Actress at Venice; Anna Cathcart's Jubilee was immediately read as a piece Marvel intends to play for years. And bringing **Finn Jones** back as Danny Rand generated the most fan discussion of any casting choice — the first time the character has been given a clear place in a story, using the flaw of the original series (a hero who serves a system without questioning it) as the starting point of the arc rather than papering over it.

**Reception:**
Critics received it as a large, brave, sorrowful finale — an aggregate around **85%** (a step down from Legacy's 88%). Praise centered on the collapse-of-Ta-Lo sequence, Guang Bo's death — soul erased in the middle of the platform, without even time to fall — the fact that Katy never once worries about Shang-Chi across the entire film, Jubilee's fireworks becoming the thing that leads four hundred people out of the dust, and the scene where he returns to a camp that has already finished grieving him. Almost all of the dissent aimed at the last three minutes.

**James Hwang, in his own words (press interview, September 2026):**
*"When we shot Ta Lo coming down, we were destroying a set I'd lived on for nine straight months across two films. You get one take. Most of what you see on my face isn't in the script."*

**Box Office:** $1.01 billion worldwide — a figure that beat every pre-release projection, since forecasts in early 2026 sat around $700–800M on the strength of two headwinds: it opened just seven weeks after **Spider-Man: Brand New Day (31 July 2026)**, and it landed amid broader superhero fatigue in the market.

What turned the number is that Brand New Day didn't absorb the demand — it created it. With Legacy having done $1.02B in 2025 and the same director's Spider-Man film clearing a billion of its own in July 2026, audiences began treating the trilogy's finale as a film to catch in theaters rather than wait out on streaming. The seven weeks between the two releases built momentum instead of draining it.

The result is an unusual trilogy arc: a capped debut at $728M (2021, China released but COVID-capped), a $1.02B peak (2025, China fully open), and **$1.01B in a saturated market (2026)** — the finale landing within a rounding error of the franchise's high point in the worst year the genre had had. It also made **Destin Daniel Cretton** one of the very few directors ever to post three consecutive billion-dollar films: Legacy, Brand New Day, and Last of the Ten Rings.

**Accolades:**
* Precursor and critics-circle nominations in visual effects, score, and production design
* Yuen Wah received supporting-actor nominations from several critics' bodies
* Destin Daniel Cretton entered the small group of directors with three consecutive billion-dollar releases — Legacy (2025), Brand New Day (2026), and Last of the Ten Rings (2026), the latter two opening seven weeks apart
* James Hwang's performance featured in multiple outlets' year-end "best of the MCU" conversations
* The trilogy as a whole was retrospectively cited as one of the most cohesive arcs of MCU Phases 4–6


## Development and production

### A finale shot before the middle film opened

*Last of the Ten Rings* is unusual among franchise finales in that it was in the can before the film preceding it had earned a dollar. It was greenlit in 2023 alongside *Legacy*, developed from the same locked script package, and shot as the back half of a single continuous block — which means every creative decision in the ending was made without any knowledge of how audiences would respond to *Legacy*.

Marvel accepted that exposure for economics: one crew, one set build, one cast contract across two films. The finale also carried the heaviest visual effects load in the trilogy, and shooting it early bought a seventeen-month post tail that a conventional schedule could not have afforded.

The studio kept the wrap secret for five months. It was disclosed publicly for the first time on **30/08/2025**, alongside the release date lock — news fans immediately read as proof the endgame had been planned years in advance.

### Comic sources and why

The finale escalates from organisational power to cosmology, and the sources follow.

| Source | What it gave the film | Why it was chosen |
|---|---|---|
| *The Immortal Iron Fist* | The hidden city whose ruler becomes a tyrant, the K'un-Lun structure | Provided a mythic architecture for a finale that had to be larger than a family drama |
| *Agents of Atlas* | Xian, Mr. Lao, and the Makluan dragons serving heaven | Source for the celestial pantheon the finale confronts |
| *Chaos War* | A mortal challenging an entire pantheon | The finale's central escalation — the scale problem solved by an existing comics precedent rather than invention |
| *Shang-Chi* (2020) | Shang-Chi as leader of an organisation, not merely a fighter | Continuity from *Legacy*; the finale resolves what that leadership costs |

### Pre-production — October 2023 to June 2024

Shared with *Legacy*. Both scripts were locked before day one, which is what makes a back-to-back block possible at all: the schedule is built by set and location rather than by film, so a stage might shoot a *Legacy* scene in the morning and a finale scene in the afternoon.

Sixteen standing sets served both films, several dressed twice to show the same location at different points in the story's history. The god-realm environments unique to the finale were designed during this window but built later, in the second half of the block, once the *Legacy* sets could be struck.

### Principal photography — December 2024 to March 2025

The finale's own material ran from December 2024, immediately after *Legacy* wrapped on 14/11/2024, through to March 2025.

| Unit | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Main unit | Disney Studios Australia, Sydney | God-realm sets, the Ten Rings compound redressed, the collapse sequences |
| Second unit | New South Wales regional | Ta Lo exteriors carried over from the *Legacy* build |
| Motion capture | Sydney volume stage | Dweller-in-Darkness and celestial-pantheon performance capture |

**Bill Pope** returned on ARRI Alexa LF with Panavision anamorphic, matched across all three films. **Andy Cheng** supervised stunts with **Alan Tang** on the second fight unit, carrying over from *Legacy* without a break.

This is the most fight-heavy film of the three, and James shot it while flying between the Sydney stages and Asian tour dates for NCT 127's "Neo City — The Momentum" from January 2025. The production scheduled around his show nights for four straight months. He has described it as the closest he came to breaking, and it is the reason the whole of late 2025 and 2026 was kept clear for music.

### Post-production — April 2025 to August 2026

A seventeen-month post tail, the longest of any film in the trilogy, opened in April 2025 while *Legacy* was still four months from release.

- **Editorial** — **Nat Sanders** and **Harry Yoon**, cutting from April 2025
- **Visual effects** — **Weta FX** on the celestial pantheon and the dimensional collapse, **Framestore** on the Ten Rings at full power, **ILM** on the god-realm environments, **Rodeo FX** and **Scanline VFX** on destruction and water. The collapse of an entire dimension made this the heaviest VFX load in the trilogy and is the single reason the post schedule ran as long as it did.
- **Score** — **Joel P West**, completing the trilogy
- **Additional photography** — **January to February 2026**, deliberately scheduled to sit half a year after *Legacy* had opened so the ending could be adjusted against real audience response. It was also the only window Cretton had free between *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* wrapping on 19/12/2025 and that film's own post.
- **Final pass** — Cretton delivered *Brand New Day* in June 2026 and had two months left to close out the trilogy's ending before the 18 September release. Several critics pointed at the last three minutes as the part most visibly made in the narrowest window.

The image of a billion-dollar film's lead singing with his group the night his own film opened became one of the most-shared moments of the year, and Marvel's marketing team ultimately built the final week of the campaign around it rather than against it.

*Spider-Man: Brand New Day* had opened on 31/07/2026 and cleared a billion dollars worldwide; the wave it generated raised expectations for the finale rather than draining them.
